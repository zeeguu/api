THE ONE RULE ABOVE ALL OTHERS
Everything said in {target_language} is what native speakers would actually say in this situation: every sentence, and every exchange, where each line is a natural answer to the line before it. And it means exactly what the Teacher says it means. The learner will copy all of it and cannot tell what is wrong from what is right, so a lesson with one unnatural sentence or exchange is worse than a shorter lesson without it. Everything below is in service of this rule; where anything below would push you to break it, do less instead.

HOW THE LESSON WORKS
This lesson follows the method of Pimsleur's first lessons: very few new words, each heard and recalled many times, spread across the whole lesson.

1. THE DIALOGUE is given, below. Use it exactly as it is: do not change, shorten or extend it. The whole lesson is built around it. Play it at the start, after the Teacher sets the scene, and tell the learner they will understand it by the end of the lesson. Play it again at the very end. To play it, write the line [dialogue]: do not write the dialogue out.

{dialogue}

   The learner plays the {learner_role}. They learn to SAY the {learner_role}'s lines, and to UNDERSTAND the other person's lines, as in real life: you must understand what the shopkeeper asks, but you do not need to say it yourself.

2. THE NEW WORDS are what the dialogue contains that is not in the table above, function words included; some are best taught as short fixed chunks (e.g. "jeg forstår ikke"). They are of two kinds:
   - words TO SAY: those in the {learner_role}'s lines;
   - words TO UNDERSTAND: those only in the other person's lines.
   All other sentences in the lesson use only the new words and the words in the table.

3. TEACHING THEM, in the order the dialogue needs them:
   - Each word TO SAY: the Teacher says in {source_language} what it means and when you say it. Then the word, once, marked [drill]: it will be said slowly and then at normal speed, with time to repeat it. Always the whole word: the voices are synthesized, and a piece of a word comes out as a wrong sound. Then the Teacher asks the learner to say it ("How do you say ...?", asked in {source_language}), marked [ask], and the answer, once, marked [answer].
   - Each line TO UNDERSTAND: the Teacher says in {source_language} what the other person will say and what it means. The other person says the line once, marked [understand]: it will be said slowly and then at normal speed. The learner does not repeat it.

4. RECALL: after each new item, the Teacher asks for one or two things learned earlier, in this lesson or in the earlier ones. Two kinds of recall:
   - for words TO SAY, about {recalls_to_say} times over the lesson, at growing distances: the Teacher's prompt in {source_language}, marked [ask], then the answer, marked [answer];
   - for lines TO UNDERSTAND, about {recalls_to_understand} times: the other person says the line, as a plain line, then the Teacher asks the learner to answer it as the {learner_role} would, marked [ask], then the answer, marked [answer]. This is how the lesson ends up practising the whole exchange.

5. NEW SENTENCES: where the words allow it, ask the learner for short sentences that are not in the dialogue but that someone in the situation would say ("Ask her whether she understands English"). With only a few words there are only a few such sentences; then recall more and combine less.

6. THE EARLIER LESSONS: words from the most recent lessons in the table come back {earlier_words_recalls} in the recalls of step 4, words from older lessons now and then, so they are not forgotten. When they belong to another situation, the Teacher sets that situation in one sentence ("You are at the café again. Order a coffee."). If the table is empty, this is the first lesson.

7. REHEARSAL: before the end, the learner rehearses the dialogues of their earlier lessons listed below, newest first: every one of them. For each, the Teacher sets that situation in one sentence ("Remember the bus station? You are buying a ticket again. Answer the woman."), and then write the line [respond N], with N the lesson's number: the dialogue is then played for the learner to answer, in the role they had in that lesson, and once straight through. Do not write the dialogue out. When the list says there are none, skip this step.

{earlier_dialogues}

8. THE END: the Teacher tells the learner, in {source_language}, to listen to the conversation again and notice that they understand it now. Then the dialogue from step 1 once more.

THE TEACHER
- Speaks {source_language}, and says more than in lessons for advanced learners: about half of the lesson. Short sentences, warm and calm.
- No grammar explanations. At most one short sentence about a word when it really helps ("this is also what you say to get someone's attention").
- When the Teacher needs to say a {target_language} word inside a {source_language} sentence, use a separate TeacherL2 line for it.
- Every challenge makes clear WHO speaks and to WHOM: "Ask the waiter...", "Say that you...", "Tell her that you...". The learner must never have to guess whether "you" means themselves or someone else.
